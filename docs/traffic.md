# The traffic simulator (cSC4TrafficSimulator)

The fourth simulator, after pollution, power and water ([pollution.md](pollution.md), [power.md](power.md), [water.md](water.md)), the same way: function by function on the original object under the parity harness ([parity.md](parity.md)). Build 641 addresses throughout.

### The step

0x007215C0 runs on every 0x66956814: on a new day of the month (simulator service slot 6), on the 1st, last month's incomes (+0x780, +0x784) go to two budget line items (department 2 item 2, department 1 item 5; their sum is slot 18's); every +0xC48 / +0xC4C days the lots of the next bucket of the multimap at +0x1A0 get a random 0-99 in property 0x4A611773. Then, until its time budget (0x0068BF50 with 4) is spent, the sub-step 0x00720F00.

The sub-step calls the function of the current phase (+0x814, 20 phases; 1, 9, 12 and 14 have none and are done at once), and when it is done moves to the next phase from the table at 0xAB3480 (12 becomes 13 unless +0x62 is set) and starts it (restarting cell iterators and walks, clearing totals; phase 19 copies this month's counts to last month's and collects the cells of the occupants of type 9). The phases 16-18 work through a count in parts; the step returns after a part. Phases 17 and 18 route trips through the path finder (0x0071C6C0, 0x0070EC40) that NAM modifies; they stay the game's for now. The time-sliced phases keep their own deadline steps (0xB4B300 and on: a step, a counter and a once flag each) and adapt them as 0x0068B960 does; integers from floats use the game's 1.5 x 2^23 addition (`MagicRound`).

### The month

0x00718AC0: the two departments' funding move a level by the curve at +0xD20 (0x00717D30, 0x00717E00: clamped to [0, 1]; that share of a count is active, the change through 0x00715FB0), the strike check (0x0070DBA0: a state at +0x7A4; underfunded department 2 starts a strike for up to +0xB9C months, messages 0x69247DC3-6), a random event in view (0x007184E0, on a stopwatch), then last month's figures (IDs 0xEA5BA01D, 0x8A5D4116, 0xABB8FAAC-0xABB8FAB4, 0xEC0BDBF8).

**Registers the game expects kept.** The game's whole-program build lets two callers keep using ECX after calling a traffic function: 0x007206BD calls 0x007183B0 right after ClearRoadEdges (0x0070FCE0), and 0x006D9404 calls 0x006D6780 right after the path finder's BuildDistanceTables (0x006D6040), neither setting ECX again. Both replacements are hooked through thunks that keep ECX and EDX (found 2026-10-09; [mysim-agents.md](mysim-agents.md) has the check). Parity with the thunks: 12 of 12, 3 of 3, 2 of 2 (90 days).

## The Network Addon Mod

NAM patches the traffic code in the middle of functions (its DLL, [patch-census.md](patch-census.md) section 9): 0x0070EAE7 in 0x0070EAB0, 0x00714222 in 0x007140E0, 0x0071AB60 in 0x0071A860, 0x00729FFF in 0x00729F90, and the pathfinder at 0x006D79BF, 0x006D7EBE/0x006D7EF0 and 0x006D95B1 (0x006D7960, 0x006D7E50, 0x006D91B0). Dan's call (2026-10-08): remake the base game's traffic exactly, and later offer a choice between vanilla, NAM and an improved model of our own. So these functions are replaced too: SetWay, OnNetworkBuilding and OnCellsChanged (0x0070EAB0, 0x007140E0, 0x0071A860) in `simulators.traffic.helpers`, and the path finder's three in `simulators.traffic.pathfinder`. With either on, a function NAM has patched stays the game's: the hook layer finds NAM's jump at the site and leaves that function alone ([architecture.md](architecture.md), "Patches inside replaced functions"), so NAM keeps working and the rest of the replacement still applies. Our own code calls those functions through the game's entry (SetWay too, from OnCellsChanged), so it reaches NAM's version. 0x00729F90 is not replaced. Mods also retarget call sites inside callers, and replacing a caller drops such a patch: the census lists none in the other replaced functions.

### Tuning

LoadTuning (0x00716AE0) reads the exemplar 0x6534284A, 0xE7E2C2DB, 0xC9133286; Init calls it, and message 0xC53D10AA calls it again when that exemplar changes. Without the exemplar it does nothing (not even LoadTravelStrategies). It reads:

- link speeds by travel type (properties 0x491332E7 + type, up to 13 by building type) into +0x980 [9][13], as speed x 16.67 / the city's cell width (slot 91), and their reciprocals into the cost tables at 0xB4B110 (0 and FLT_MAX for no link);
- the travel time curve (0xB4B2F0, property 0xA92356B9) with its y values inverted, and +0xC58 = 255 / (its last y - 1);
- the reciprocals of 0xA92356BB into the float vector at +0xC6C (count at +0xC68), and 0xA92356B3 with its reciprocals (+0xBCC, +0xC00; 1 below 1e-10);
- flags by travel type from three bool arrays (+0xC34, 0xB4B100, +0xC3D; true past the array's end);
- four curves (+0xD10, +0xD20, +0xD30, +0xD40) with defaults when the exemplar has none, and some 20 single values.

The property vectors are the game's own (the getters 0x005FFCC0 and 0x005FF000 fill them, 0x004641B0 and 0x007C61A0 grow them) and are freed in the game's order, so the heap sees the same allocations. Quirk kept: 0xA92356B1 copies nine words whatever the array's length. `OPENSC4_TRAFFIC_SELFCHECK=1` runs the game's function, then ours, and compares everything both write.

### The path finder

Not a GZCOM class: vtable 0x00AB2150 (34 slots, interface 0xAA0DB766), constructor 0x006D89C0, made by the simulator's slot 6 (0x0070D860) for +0x830 and +0x834. RouteTrip sets it up through its setters (flags +0x70, mode +0x80, goal types +0x64/+0x66, rectangles +0x18 and +0x28, count +0x68, cost limits +0x90, budget +0xC0) and runs slot 23, Search (0x006D91B0): A* over (cell, side entered, travel type) nodes. A node is 0x24 bytes (key, parent, g, f, open/closed, side, type, x, z, hash link, bucket, heap index), from pages of 0x24000 bytes kept in a 0xC07-bucket hash at +0x128; the open set is a binary heap on f at +0x114.

From each node Search opens the neighbours its cell's record allows (ways by type and side, a switch to another type through the record's nibbles, the masks +0x64 and, during a strike, 0x33), with the link cost of the edge's kind (through the travel time curve at the cell's traffic for the types of 0xB4B100) or a route id's cost (0x0071CB20), ferry entries (the hash at simulator +0xC8), transit links (+0xDC) and an exit beside the road at +0xEE/+0xF0. A step costing more than the type's limit (cost limits + +0x60) counts against the type, which is blocked after 64. The estimate is the distance in cells to the goal rectangle (tables at +0x10C, +0x110) x +0x14. Goals by mode (0x006D7960): 0 a building beside the cell with room for the trip's kinds (lots found by rings around the source, 0x006D7E50), 2 an accepting network object near the cell, 3 the goal rectangle. The search runs under a sliced deadline (step at 0xB49B34), returns 2 when out of time and resumes (+0xC4). Slot 32 (0x006D7070) encodes the route as bytes for the trip: the start (type, x, z), runs of 2-bit steps (type | 0x40, count, steps four a byte), jumps (type | 0x80, x, z); slot 30 checks a kept route against the records.

`simulators.traffic.pathfinder` replaces Search, the pool and heap, the estimate and distance tables, the start nodes, the goal checks and setup, the route's finish, encoding and check, and Reset; the constructor, setters and slot 29 stay the game's. NAM patches Search (0x006D95B1), the goal check (0x006D79BF) and the goal setup (0x006D7EBE, 0x006D7EF0): with NAM's DLL loaded those three stay the game's (with NAM's patches) and the rest of this replacement is ours.

### The water route finder

At the simulator's +0x108; vtable 0x00AB34FC (QueryInterface and AddRef shared, Release 0x00725130, DoMessage 0x007249D0), constructor 0x007250F0, a node pool of 0x1C000-byte pages in 0xC07 buckets (from +0xC). Init (0x00725490) makes a uint8 sim grid of water depths (built inline: vtables 0x00A878C0/0x00A878A4) at +0x3044 and registers for six messages; terrain changes (0x278E6003, 0x678E6E3A; the rectangle from the message's two words, its low corner one cell further) and buildings inserted or removed whose cell the terrain (0x00B43CF4 slot 34) accepts grow a dirty rectangle (+0x3048, flag +0x3058); CityInited rebuilds every cell, 0x6A3B0AFD the dirty ones (0x00724840): 0 off water, else the depth (at most 40) x 5 rounded up (the game's round(v + 0.5), one less for a whole v). The search (0x00725170, behind GetWaterRoute): between two cells of the same body of water (0x00B43CF0 slot 9's map), A* over the depth grid with a sorted open list (+0x3040) and 0x1C-byte nodes, a step costing (40 - depth x 0.2) x 0.1 + 1 and the straight distance as the estimate; the routes there and back as route bytes (0x00724C50: type 0, the start cell, runs of steps).

### Our object

`simulators.traffic.object` registers our factory under the class ID 0x6990C1AA, as power and water do: the game's layout (0xD58 bytes), our constructor and destructor (the members built and torn down through the game's container code in the game's order: hash sets of 100 buckets, the tree headers, the stopwatch, the string, the curves, the vectors of 9 and the reserves of 100), QueryInterface (0x68027F37 and 1 at +0, 0x452294AA at +4, 0xE4FDA3D4 at +8), AddRef, Release (Shutdown and the deleting destructor at the last reference), and copies of the three vtables taken when the first object is made, so every other method is the game's or ours as configured. Turn it on with every other traffic replacement.

### Clock reads in CityInited

CityInited runs phases 13, 5 and 10 to completion under deadlines that never pass, made from an `rdtsc` read each time round. Those three reads are sites the virtual clock traps, and each trapped read advances it, so our version reads through `virtual_clock::ReadTsc`, which does what the trap does (same value, count and trace). Skipping or adding a read would shift every later clock value.

### The save record

`tools/re/sc4save.py` decodes version 41 (`traffic_simulator`; every record the game writes now), and the parity harness compares it field by field. The shipped tutorial cities are version 40; Read takes both. Offsets in the game's Write and Read are from the cIGZSerializable part at object +0x8. Two of Read's quirks are kept: one-byte fields read through 0x005D9FA0 come back as 0 or 1, and a grown byte row is first filled with the low byte of its row offset before every byte is read over it.

The fingerprint's `traffic` part: the trip scale and the one shown, last month's income and the freight scaling (slots 14, 15, 18, 27), as float bits, besides the congestion, trip length, commercial and air-polluting traffic grids already in it.
