# UI scaling (P2)

The game draws its interface at its original pixel size, so it gets smaller as screens get bigger: at 3440x1440 the control panel and text are about a third of the size they were designed for on 1024x768. This document records how the game draws and positions its interface and the options for scaling it.

## How the game draws (survey, 2026-10-06)

A draw count by projection, viewport and calling function in the test city (D3D11 driver, 1024x768):

- **Interface panels** all draw through one function (the call returning to 0x00993242). Each window draws with the viewport set to its own screen rectangle (buttons 68x30, the tutorial dialog 768x600) and a pixel-exact orthographic projection for that size, y pointing down. So the driver can tell interface draws apart from the city.
- **The city** is drawn by other callers (returns to 0x007D29BB, 0x007D29E5, 0x007D2A09, 0x007D4B1C) and the terrain through the dynamic vertex buffers, with the full-screen viewport and the camera's orthographic projection.
- **Full-screen 2D** from 0x007AA0AF: a pixel projection over the whole screen (about 120,000 draws in 15 s), presumably overlays drawn over the city view.
- **A second 3D view** inside a panel (508x358, its own projection), presumably a preview or the map.

The window system (gzcom-dll's `cIGZWin`, `cIGZWinMgr`, `cISC4View3DWin`): windows have areas in screen pixels (`GetArea`, `SetArea`, `SetSize`) inside their parent; the window manager owns a canvas, finds windows under a point (`GetWindowFromPoint`), and takes mouse input through one entry point, `GZWinMsgPost_Mouse(message, x, z, flags)`, plus `GetCursorScreenPosition` and `SetCursorPosition`. The 3D view window picks terrain and occupants from screen coordinates (`PickTerrain`, `PickOccupant`). The game also calls `GetCursorPos`/`ScreenToClient` (0x0098AF00) and `SetCursorPos`/`ClientToScreen` (0x005C4480, 0x009DA9A4) itself.

## Options

**A. Render scale** (the roadmap's interim item). The game runs at the screen size divided by a scale factor (3440x1440 / 1.5 = 2293x960) and the D3D11 driver draws it at the full size. Everything gets bigger: interface, text and city. The city is drawn at full resolution (its projections do not depend on pixel size), so edges stay sharp; interface images and text are scaled up and a little soft. The visible part of the city is what a 2293x960 screen shows.

Work: the driver keeps a full-size back buffer and scales everything the game expresses in pixels (viewports, clears, buffer regions, front-buffer reads and snapshots, which go back to the game at its size); the mode list offers the scaled sizes; mouse messages are divided by the factor in the driver's window procedure, and the game's own `GetCursorPos`, `SetCursorPos`, `ClientToScreen` and `ScreenToClient` calls are translated (import hooks). Days, and every piece is plain driver or Win32 work.

**B. Interface-only scaling** (the roadmap's "proper" item). The city view keeps the full resolution and visible area; only the panels grow. The driver side is the same as A, applied only to interface draws (which it can recognise, see above). The game side needs the 3D view to render and pick at the full size while the window system lays panels out at the scaled size: the view window's size as the renderer and camera see it, its picking coordinates, and the mouse coordinates it receives would all be scaled. That means reverse engineering how `cISC4View3DWin` passes its size to the renderer and camera and how the full-screen 2D overlays relate to it. Weeks, with the size uncertain until that is understood.

A is a strict subset of B's work: the driver scaling and the input translation are needed by both. B adds the game-side hooks for the 3D view.

## The city at full size (option B, the default since 2026-10-06)

B turned out much smaller than feared, because it can sit on top of A instead of beside it. With A, the game already lives in a smaller pixel space and the driver draws the city at the physical size; only the camera decides how much of the city a game pixel covers. That is one function, the city camera's projection setup (`cSC4CameraControl::UpdateProjection`, our name, 0x007CBE40):

- pixels per 16 m tile for the zoom level (this+0x108) come from a table at 0x00ABACE0 (8, 16, 32, 73, 146 for zoom levels 1 to 5, with camera pitches 30, 35, 40, 45, 45 degrees at 0x00ABACCC) times a fine zoom factor at this+0xF0 (1.0 normally);
- it stores metres per pixel (this+0x134) and pixels per metre (this+0x138), and sets the orthographic box to the view's size in pixels (this+0x12C, this+0x130) times metres per pixel (0x007FF2E0);
- the table is read nowhere else, and the function is called from the zoom setters (0x007CD6E0, 0x007CDC50).

With a scale above 1, the driver wraps that function (`[renderer] scale_city = yes` turns this off and gives option A's enlarged city): it divides the fine zoom factor by the scale for the duration of the call and puts it back afterwards, so each game pixel covers scale times as much of the city. The city then comes out at the stock game's full-resolution size (a zoom-5 tile is 145 physical pixels wide; stock: 146), while the interface stays enlarged as with A. Everything that goes through what the camera stores follows on its own.

Checked unattended at 3440x1440, scale 1.5 (the game sees 2293x960):

- the whole city fits where A showed it 1.5 times as big; the interface is unchanged;
- picking: with the query tool, the highlighted tile is under the cursor at the top left, the centre and the bottom right of the screen (a projection mismatch would be off by about three tiles at the edges);
- the tutorial's camera moves centre the town; the wheel zooms; edge scrolling works and only starts on the last row of pixels; scrolling (which moves the saved picture by a pixel offset the game works out from the camera) leaves no gaps.

Not covered, or different:

- **The region view** is still drawn as with A (enlarged). It has no camera to adjust: it draws each city's saved thumbnail (resource type 0x8A2482B9, read at 0x00459B20, 0x005DCA10, 0x005DD480) as an image, and the two other users of the city camera's box setter (0x007FF2E0) are a panel-sized camera (0x004202F0) and the renderer's own setup. Showing it at native size means the region view window (`cSC4WinRegionView`, class 0x2BA6BB97, inside `cSC4WinRegionScreen`, 0xEA659793) laying itself out and taking mouse input in physical pixels while the rest of the interface stays scaled. See "Text and the region view" below.
- Text and interface images are still the scaled-up 1x art (see "Interface images" below); crisp text needs the fonts drawn at the larger size.
- Zoning lands where dragged: a residential zone dragged between two screen points starts and ends on the tiles under the cursor, with its street. Not tried yet: the other 3D views in panels, performance in a big city (the screen shows 2.25 times as much city at 1.5), and how scrolling speed feels.


## Render scale (option A, implemented 2026-10-06)

`opensc4.ini` `[renderer]` `scale = 1.5` (1 to 4, D3D11 driver only). The driver lists every mode at its physical size divided by the scale, and draws the game's frames at the physical size: viewports, scissors, clears and buffer-region copies are converted to physical pixels (edges rounded, so neighbouring rectangles stay neighbours); snapshots (`CopyColorBuffer`) read the physical pixels and return the game's size (nearest). The city's projections do not depend on pixel size, so it is drawn sharp; interface images and text are scaled up.

**The game sees a desktop scaled down by the same factor**, the way Windows virtualises coordinates for programs that are not DPI aware. Translating only our window's client coordinates was not enough: the window layer keeps its client area as a screen rectangle (0x0098D9B0) and takes the screen's size as its own in full screen (0x0098ADE0). So the game's imports of `ScreenToClient`, `ClientToScreen`, `MapWindowPoints`, `GetClientRect`, `GetWindowRect`, `GetWindowPlacement`, `MoveWindow`, `SetWindowPos`, `GetCursorPos`, `SetCursorPos`, `SystemParametersInfoA` (work area), `GetSystemMetrics` (screen size), `EnumDisplaySettingsA` and `GetDeviceCaps` (screen size) are wrapped in the exe's import table ([iat.h](../src/core/iat.h)), so other modules see Windows as it is; our window's messages (client coordinates, and the screen coordinates of `WM_MOVE` and the wheel) are divided in the window procedure. The game asks about the window while `CreateWindowEx` is still running (`WM_CREATE`, the first `WM_SIZE`), so the window procedure marks it as the scaled one on its first message; before that fix the game laid out a 3440x1440 interface inside its 2293x960 picture and clipped the tutorial panel. The wrappers go in when the driver registers (DirectorInit), before the game measures the screen.

With a scale, the ini decides the size: `resolution` in physical pixels (default: the desktop's), `window` (default full screen); the game's saved size would match no mode, which crashes it.

Checked unattended: 3440x1440 at scale 1.5 (game 2293x960) draws the city, the control panel and the tutorial panel at 1.5 times their size; 1920x1080 windowed at 1.5 (game 1280x720) runs; scale 1 still matches the stock driver in all 20 shots. Mouse input, checked unattended with synthesized input on 2026-10-06 (3440x1440, scale 1.5, full screen): clicks land on the button under the cursor (tutorial, Mayor and Budget panels, region view tiles and city card), tooltips appear by the cursor, the wheel zooms, edge scrolling works on the right and left edges, and right-dragging pans. Edge scrolling first showed the buffer-region bug described in [renderer.md](renderer.md) ("What the driver calls mean"), which was in the driver at every scale, not in the scaling. Still to try by hand: how it feels, and tools that place things (zoning, roads).

**Interface images.** Sampled as the game sets them (mostly nearest), they came out uneven at non-integer scales: some strokes one pixel wide, some two. Interface draws are recognised by their pixel-exact projection (one unit per pixel of the viewport, which the city's projection never is), and their nearest-filtered images are drawn with sharp bilinear filtering: each texel stays a flat square, with a band of about a pixel blended at its edges, the pixels-per-texel worked out from screen-space derivatives so stretched images work too. Strokes now have an even weight with soft edges. The glyphs themselves are thin, anti-aliased 1x images, so text stays light; crisp text means the game drawing its fonts (TrueType outlines, the `.mxf` files XOR-ed with 0x9D) at the larger size, which belongs with option B.

## Text and the region view (survey, 2026-10-06)

Both are drawn on the CPU. A temporary log of the game functions that create and fill textures (in the test city), plus dumps of what they upload, showed three sources:

- 3D textures for the city (created at 0x0080A22D, filled at 0x00809C8D, all mip levels);
- a few images from 0x006048A8/0x006048FC;
- **CPU-drawn pictures** kept as textures by one class (vtable around 0x00ADB2F4/0x00ADB308): it wraps a picture in memory, splits it into tiles of 16 to 256 pixels (0x00A6CF00), creates one texture per tile (0x00991440, driver call at 0x0099155B), and uploads either everything (0x00991285) or only the rectangles that changed (0x00991336, from 0x00990FF0).

The changed rectangles are the interface's text windows: a dump showed "Timbuktu", "Region" and "29,156" drawn together with the panel's background gradient, at game-pixel size, plus the arrow sprite. The 256x256 tiles are the region view: one large picture of the region composed from the city thumbnails. The game's text engine is the framework's fonts over T2K, a third-party font scaler (no GDI font calls), which draw into those pictures; [fonts.md](fonts.md) maps it and lists the hook points for crisp text.

So crisp text and a native-size region view are the same piece of work: the game's CPU-side 2D drawing (text, image blits, fills into these pictures) would have to happen at the physical resolution, with the pictures scale times larger and the windows that own them still laid out in game pixels. The driver alone cannot do it, because by the time a picture reaches it the text is already pixels. Possible routes, none started:

1. Make the pictures larger and scale every 2D drawing call into them (the game's canvas/blit layer and the text rasterizer's size). Needs that layer reverse engineered; its extent is not known yet.
2. Leave the pictures alone and, per text draw, render the same string again at the larger size into a shadow picture that the driver uses instead. Only text, but needs the text draw calls (string, font, size, position) and the mapping from picture to texture tiles.

Either is weeks rather than days, with the size uncertain until the 2D layer is mapped. Route 2 is underway: see "Sharp interface tiles" below.

## Sharp interface tiles (crisp text, first slice, 2026-10-26)

Route 2 above needs the text windows' textures to hold more pixels than the game's pictures. That half is done; the other half, drawing the text into them, is the next slice.

**How a picture reaches the screen.** A CPU-drawn picture (`cIGZBuffer`) gets a textures object (`UIPictureTextures`, 0x68 bytes, constructor 0x009916E0, primary vtable 0x00ADB2E0) from the window that draws it (0x00992F80 for windows, 0x0098F110 for the picture draw itself; the picture keeps it at its slots 0x94 and 0x98). The object's Create (0x00991440, slot 9) splits the picture into tiles of 16 to 256 pixels (0x00A6CF00) and asks the graphics driver for one texture each (its slot 0xF0, the format by the picture's type); Upload (0x00990FF0) sends the changed rectangles (the driver's LoadTextureLevel, slot 0xF4, game-sized, straight from the picture's memory or through the colour-key copies of [pixels.md](pixels.md)). The draw (0x0098F110) asks the object for each tile's texture and size (secondary vtable 0x00ADB2C8, slot 5) and builds quads whose texture coordinates are pixel positions times one over the tile's size. So a texture larger than the size the object reports fills exactly the same quad.

**What the slice does.** `renderer.interface.sharp_tiles` (off by default; [src/core/ui_sharp_tiles.cpp](../src/core/ui_sharp_tiles.cpp)) wraps Create in a `gd::PhysicalTextures` scope. Under a render scale the D3D11 driver then makes those textures scale times larger (rounded up), keeps a game-sized BGRA copy of each, writes every upload into the copy and fills the physical pixels over the rectangle (and one around it) from it: each physical pixel is the average of the game pixels it covers, weighted by how much of it each covers and by their alpha (transparent pixels lend no colour). That is what the driver's sharp filtering does on the GPU for a game-sized texture, so the picture looks the same; the difference is that its tiles can now take pixels drawn at the physical size. With a scale of 1, or without the D3D11 driver, nothing changes.

Checked on TurboToaster (2026-10-26), Making Money, D3D11 driver, windowed 1280x960 at scale 1.25 (the game sees 1024x768; 1536x1152 at 1.5 does not fit the 1920x1080 screen and the game quits at start): the 20 game-sized shots with the group on match those without (`--tolerance 32 --max-differing 1.2`); at full resolution (`OPENSC4_TEST_SHOT_FULL=1`, new: each shot also saved at the physical size, as `z<zoom>-r<rotation>-full.png`), the tutorial panel's text is the same to the eye, 20% of its pixels a little different (at most 83 of 255 in a channel, from filtering on the CPU instead of the GPU), so it does go through the larger tiles. At scale 1 with the D3D11 driver, 20 of 20 shots match.

**The text itself (slice 2, done for pictures with tiles: "Sharp interface text" below).** Our DrawString (f3g, [fonts.md](fonts.md)) knows the picture, the font, the string, the colours, the clip and every placed glyph. The plan:

1. The glyphs at scale times the font's size from FreeType, which reads the game's own faces (the `.mxf` files hold Type 1 fonts as PFB files, scrambled) and renders real outlines with current anti-aliasing and hinting on Windows, macOS and Linux alike (a native port needs a replacement for T2K anyway; DirectWrite would tie this to Windows). Where FreeType cannot open a face, T2K at the larger size (a copy of the font with SetSize).
2. For each DrawString into a picture that has a textures object, the glyphs placed at the physical positions of the 1x pen (the 1x pen times the scale, so lines and layout stay where the game put them), composited the way the f4 blitter composites (its blend tables and modes, the ramp in mode 2) into a physical-size overlay of the text's rectangle, over the physical background. The background: the rectangle as it was before DrawString drew (read from the picture before the blitter runs), filtered up as the driver does.
3. The overlay kept per picture with the text's rectangle and a hash of the 1x pixels it produced; at Upload, a physical rectangle whose 1x pixels still match takes the overlay's pixels instead of the filtered ones (the driver asks the game side through a hook in UploadScaled); a rectangle the game has drawn over since (the hash no longer matches) drops its overlay.

Open questions for slice 2: whether pictures are drawn to again between DrawString and Upload by blits that copy text (scrolling lists), which would need the overlay to follow the copy; how many fonts the game makes (one more font per size used); the region view's city names (drawn into the region picture, 256-pixel tiles: the same path).

## Sharp interface text (crisp text, second slice, 2026-10-26)

`renderer.interface.sharp_text` (off by default; [src/core/ui_sharp_text.cpp](../src/core/ui_sharp_text.cpp)) draws each line of text a second time at the physical size, with FreeType ([third_party/freetype](../third_party), FreeType License, portable to macOS and Linux). It needs `renderer.interface.sharp_tiles` and our DrawString (`framework.font.font.draw`, [fonts.md](fonts.md) f3g), and acts only under a render scale and for 32-bit pictures.

- **Faces.** FreeType opens the font's own file (its path, +0x0C; a bare name is looked for in Windows's Fonts folder, as the font system does). The game's `.mxf` faces are Type 1 fonts as PFB files behind the "MXFN" header and its XOR scramble, so FreeType's Type 1 driver reads them once they are unscrambled; the TrueType and CFF drivers take Windows's faces and OpenType ones. A face FreeType cannot open, or a line with a character the face lacks, stays as the game draws it.
- **A line's copy.** Our DrawString tells the module each glyph it keeps (its pen and box). Just before the blitter draws, the module copies the line's rectangle from the picture, with two pixels around it (a colour-keyed picture's key pixels cleared to 0, as Upload's copy does). Just after, it hashes what the line left in the rectangle and makes the physical-size copy: the copied background filtered up with the driver's filter (`gd::FilterUp`), then each character rendered by FreeType at the font's size times the scale (light hinting, anti-aliased) on the pen the game used, times the scale, and the baseline (the pen's y plus the line spacing +0x78 and the ascent +0x84). So the layout stays the game's, and the colours compose as the blitter's kind does (1 solid above half coverage, 2 the ramp's colour, 3 blended by the coverage). Copies are kept per picture (at most 256; a new one replaces any it covers).
- **Into the tiles.** The module notes the textures object while its Upload runs (a detour of 0x00990FF0). The driver calls `gd::SetScaledUploadHook`'s hook with every scaled tile upload. The hook finds the tile's place in the picture from the object's pieces (0x00A6CD10: the picture's x and y against the tile's), drops copies whose rectangle no longer hashes the same, and writes the others over the filtered pixels.

Checked on TurboToaster (2026-10-26, D3D11 driver, 1280x960 at scale 1.25). The region view's city names (drawn straight into the region picture, which has tiles) come out as FreeType's outlines at the physical size: crisp, the letters a little wider apart (each on the game's 1x pen times 1.25). The font test (`OPENSC4_TEST_FONTS`, stock driver, no scale) gives the same hashes with all three groups on (fontdraw 469241D2). At scale 1 (D3D11), 20 of 20 shots match without them; at 1.25, the 20 game-sized shots match within the usual tolerance. New for checking: `OPENSC4_TEST_REGION_FULL=<file>` saves the region view at its physical size when `OPENSC4_TEST_REGION_HOLD` ends.

**Not yet: text in panels.** Most windows draw their text into pictures of their own that have no textures object (the tutorial panel, the region screen's "Region" and "Timbuktu" labels). Those pictures are then composited into the picture that has tiles, through the drawing context: cIGZBuffer's Blt (slot 0x74, 0x0082B690 for vtable 0x00AC1550) hands the copy to its context's slot 0x98, which is the software rasterizer of the "uncharted" range (class vtable 0x00AC16C8). The copies stay with the first picture and never reach a tile. Slice 3: follow copies through those image draws. For a draw that is a plain translation, move the copy with it: shift its pixels when the offset times the scale is whole, else render it again at the new place. Where the source is transparent, compose it over the target's own pixels from before the draw, filtered up. Then hash it in the target. A stretched draw drops the copy.
